IVS appends an fMP4 stream's fragments to one SourceBuffer for both tracks, with `timestampOffset` set to the stream's own time. At each still the SourceBuffer ended 0.1 to 1.2 s past the playhead, while the player counted 6.5 to 13 s of buffer and played at 1.03 to bring its latency down. Closing about 2.6 s at 3% takes 88 s.

## Where it starts

In `t827-ext` the latency was 1.8 to 2.1 s until the end of the midroll. At 14:44:41, at the first poll where Purple gave the player the page's playlist again (untouched, "Stream is free"), the buffer the player counted went from 1.5 to 7.6 s. The SourceBuffer did not grow. The player moved to 1.03 at once, and its latency read 8.0 s six seconds later; from then on it never came back under 7 s and reached 15 s.

The backups of that break were numbered 3 ahead (F-27, `sequenceShifted` 3). The page's playlist after the break numbered the stream further ahead, as on `/channel-d` (B-054, [same segment twice](2026-10-31-same-segment-twice.md#with-f-27-the-ends-of-the-breaks)), so it listed the last segments the player had under the numbers after its last. On an fMP4 stream the player wrote them over themselves, at their own timestamps, and counted them as new: the 6.1 s its buffer grew by, about 3 segments, were not in the SourceBuffer. On a TS stream the same segments fetched again put the video behind the audio.

`t827-ext` fetched 4 `.mp4` URIs more than once (27, 27, 9 and 5 times), against 9 URIs 2 or 3 times in `t827-rec`. In `t827-f28`, which recorded the playlists, the URI fetched again at each switch between the page's playlist and a backup was the `EXT-X-MAP` init segment.
